The Nick Virgilio Haiku Association, with 20+ years of experience, operates the Writers House in Waterfront South. NVHA is a non-profit organization that celebrates poetry and its impact on communities. Based in Camden, NJ, we offer workshops, readings, and writing sessions for all ages, fostering creativity and education, while honoring Nick Virgilio's legacy and inspiring lives through the art of poetry.

## Mission statement

Our mission is to foster the creative and spiritual practice of haiku and poetry for everyone, especially youth, to enhance literacy, deepen appreciation of nature and human experience, and build connected communities. We also aim to advance the legacy of Nick Virgilio, Camden's esteemed poet, through collaborations with academic, civic, and cultural institutions.

## The Writers House

The Nick Virgilio Haiku Association is proud to be Camden's center for writers of all ages, honoring Camden's own Nick Virgilio, world renowned haiku poet. The Writers House, opened in 2018, is a community space and provides educational programs to support literacy, creative writing, and self-expression through the writing of haiku and other verse forms. We house Mighty Writers Camden to offer educational opportunities and food and diaper distribution to Camden's families. Outside, you will find a Book Ark mini library with free books for the public. [Visit the Writers House](/writers-house/).

## History and purpose

Founded in 1989, the Nick Virgilio Haiku Association (NVHA) aims to inspire and support the writing of haiku poetry, especially among young people, and to honor the legacy of Camden's own Nick Virgilio. Over the past decades, the NVHA has:

- Created the Nick Virgilio Writers House in Waterfront South, Camden, housing the literacy and food and diaper distribution programs of Mighty Writers Camden.
- Partnered with Rutgers University on the Nick Virgilio Poetry Project and created the NVHA Virgilio Archive Project.
- Established and managed an annual haiku scholarship competition for middle and high school students in partnership with the Haiku Society of America.
- Organized numerous free educational workshops on haiku writing, and begun teaching elementary, middle school and high school students haiku and other short form poetry in public, private and parochial schools.
- Operated extensive free programming for the community, including open mics, Ginko walks, the Midnight Notes readings with local poets, and virtual workshops.
- Built and maintained a social media following of nearly 4,000 followers across YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and X, as well as a base of lifetime members and annual supporters.
- Held an annual poetry reading and celebration to commemorate Nick Virgilio's birthday, and promoted haiku writing and mindful nature walks to inspire poetry.

## Nick Virgilio's legacy

Part of our mission is the preservation of Nick Virgilio's legacy. When Nick died in 1989, the NVHA was created to memorialize him as one of the first, and one of the best, writers of American haiku poetry and to give readers new ways to access the full range of his poems, published and unpublished. [Read about Nick](/nick-virgilio-poet/).

His archive, approximately 8,750 typed pages, is housed in the Special Collections department of Robeson Library at Rutgers University–Camden. Through a collaboration between NVHA and Robeson Library, it has been digitized and made available to the public free of charge, so writers, teachers, and students can compare Nick's published poems with over 20,000 early drafts and fragments.

Nick published some 800 haiku in his lifetime, mostly in limited-circulation magazines like *American Haiku*, *Modern Haiku*, and *Frogpond*. With the assistance of The Haiku Foundation, NVHA has published a collection of all of his published works, with the place and date of first publication. *Collected Haiku* is available in [our store](/store/).

## Accessibility

The Nick Virgilio Writers House is dedicated to an accessible and inclusive creative space in Camden's Waterfront South District. Whenever possible, we host readings and events in our garden, weather permitting.

- Our first floor meets ADA compliance, with full wheelchair accessibility, including a ramp and restrooms. Our second and third floors are reached by stairs, and we are seeking a grant to improve access to them.
- Assistive listening systems are available on request.
- Closed captioning is provided for our Zoom meetings and YouTube videos.
- Large print versions of our books can be ordered through print-on-demand services.
- Sign language interpretation is available at our poetry readings with prior notice.
